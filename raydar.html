Case study · Flagship build

Raydar.

An AI-assisted commerce intelligence system that turns fragmented trend, market, and product evidence into structured, explainable decisions — built for my own digital-design business.

The problem

More information wasn't the answer.

A digital-design business generates a constant stream of market signals, competitor moves, and performance data. The real constraint was never a shortage of information — it was turning those scattered inputs into one consistent decision about what deserved production time.

The system

A decision system, not a trend dashboard.

Rather than another analytics dashboard, Raydar structures evidence, applies deterministic scoring logic, explains the reasoning behind every score, and carries an approved opportunity through a complete workflow — from research through launch and performance review.

1. Signals
Market, competitor, and trend signals come in from multiple sources.
2. Evidence
Signals are structured into comparable, normalized evidence.
3. Scoring
Evidence is scored by deterministic code — not an LLM guess.
4. AI interpretation
AI summarizes evidence and surfaces patterns worth a human's attention.
5. Human approval
A person makes the call on what's worth building. The model doesn't get the final vote.
6. Pipeline
Approved opportunities move through a tracked production pipeline.
7. Launch
The product ships.
8. Performance
Real results feed back into the next round of decisions.

How it scores

Eight weighted dimensions, explained every time.

Every opportunity is scored the same way, so patterns are comparable and the reasoning behind a recommendation is never a black box.

Demand
20%

How much real buyer demand the evidence supports.

Trend momentum
15%

Whether the signal is accelerating or fading.

Repeatability
15%

Can this become a repeatable product line, not a one-off.

Customization
15%

Room for variation without diluting the core idea.

Product fit
15%

How well it fits the shop's actual capabilities.

Competition gap
10%

Room in the market versus how saturated it already is.

Confidence
10%

How strong and independent the supporting evidence is.

Saturation risk
−10%

A penalty applied when a space is already crowded.

Human-in-the-loop

Code calculates. AI interprets. Humans decide.

  • Code calculates. Numerical scoring is handled by deterministic code, not an LLM — reproducible and explainable every time.
  • AI interprets. AI operates at the interpretation layer: summarizing evidence, identifying patterns, and helping explain why an opportunity may matter.
  • Humans decide. The system organizes, compares, scores, and recommends — a person makes the final call.
"The hardest part of AI product design is deciding where AI should stop. Useful automation isn't measured by how much human judgment it removes."

Public vs. private

What's public, what stays private.

Raydar runs in two modes on the same underlying product — the public demo restricts what's visible, it doesn't diverge into a separate app.

Public demo

The interface and product logic you can click into right now, running on synthetic, anonymized data. No live LLM calls, nothing persisted.

Private operating system

The full version I actually run day-to-day — real evidence, real sources, and real shop data. Not exposed publicly.
